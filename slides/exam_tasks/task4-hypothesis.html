TASK 4: HIGHER WEIGHTS?

Forge: Historical hammers sat at 956 g. New system. Ten new weights. Your branch (ξ₁₃=2): did the mean go up?

Book: Unit 3.2 (Normal) + Unit 7.1–7.2 (hypothesis testing). Prereq: σ-ruler in ch3-distributions.

Model: Normal weights

Propose $W \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$.

Assumptions (say them in the notebook)

  • i.i.d. draws from one process
  • Roughly bell-shaped weights (CLT still helps the sample mean)
  • Historical $\mu_0=956$ g, $\sigma_0=17.2$ g — treat $\sigma$ as known for the z-path
  • Uncertain: σ may have changed; batch dependence
$\mu_0=\xi_{11}=956,\quad \sigma_0=\xi_{12}=17.2$

Hypotheses for "higher"

$H_0:\;\mu=956$
$H_1:\;\mu > 956$

One-sided because the exam asks for higher, not "different." Two-sided wastes power on the left tail.

Skipped branches

ξ₁₃=0/1/3 → variance tests. Not your path — one sentence each in the notebook.

How surprising is $\bar x$?

$z=\dfrac{\bar x-\mu_0}{\sigma_0/\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{H_0}\mathcal{N}(0,1)$

Reject at $\alpha=0.05$ if $z>1.645$ (or $p<0.05$).

Type I = false alarm. Type II = miss a real lift.

Your numbers → conclusion

ξ₁₄ (n=10): 966, 957, 1036, 902, 1014, 968, 1002, 973, 949, 946.

$\bar x=971.3$ g
$z\approx 2.813$
$p\approx 0.0025$
At α=0.05: $z=2.81>1.645$ → reject $H_0$. Evidence for higher mean weight.

Also write the critical region as the logical statement $z>1.645$. (At 1% you still reject: 2.81>2.33.)
