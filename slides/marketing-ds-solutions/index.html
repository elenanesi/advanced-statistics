How it works, in one sentence

A model learns your normal pattern (trend, seasonality, market signals) from the months before the campaign, forecasts the “no-campaign world”, and the gap is what your campaign added.

Use it when there is no A/B test

TV & audio bursts, national promos, price changes, launches — anything you can’t split-test but still need to defend in a budget review.

02 · Attribution — statistical, top-down, no user tracking needed

Which channels get the credit?

Share of modeled sales contribution, last 12 months

Why it beats last-click reports

  • Works from spend + outcome data, not cookies or user tracking — unaffected by consent loss and signal decay.
  • Captures carry-over: an ad seen today keeps driving sales for days or weeks. Click reports miss this entirely.
  • Credits upper-funnel channels (TV, video, display) that rarely get the last click but still move the number.

What you receive

A contribution split per channel with a confidence tier attached — we tell you explicitly which estimates are solid and which need more data before you act on them.

03 · Elasticity — the “what if we moved the budget?” engine

Where should the next euro go?

Response curve: conversions vs. spend (one channel)

Elasticity = the % change in the KPI when spend changes 1%. Elasticity 0.3 → +10% spend ≈ +3% KPI.

The reallocation logic

Every channel has its own curve. Money moves from flat curves (saturated channels) to steep curves (under-invested channels) until no move improves the total. That is the whole optimization, made visible.

Scenario planner

Before committing a budget shift, you type it in — “move €200K from Display to Social” — and get a projected KPI change with an uncertainty range. Test the decision before you make it.

Honest limits

  • These are observational models — directional guidance with explicit uncertainty, not lab-grade proof.

  • Every number ships with a confidence tier — we flag what’s solid and what isn’t.
